\section{Introduction}
In the Riccati recursion, states (and dynamics lagrangian multipliers) are eliminated using the dynamics equation
\begin{equation}
    f(x_k, u_k) - x_{k+1} = 0
\end{equation}
of which the linearization that appears in the constraint jacobian is
\begin{equation}
    B_k u_k + A_k x_k - x_{k+1} = 0
\end{equation}

Controls (and stage-wise equality lagrnagian multipliers) are eliminated partially using the equality constraints by decomposing the stage-wise equality constraint jacobian, given by
\begin{equation}
    H_u = P_l T_l \begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_\rho & \\ & \bm{0} \end{bmatrix} T_r P_r
\end{equation}

In the case of implicit integrators, the dynamics equation is given by
\begin{equation}
    f(x_k, u_k, x_{k+1}) = 0
\end{equation}
which can be implemented by introducing the controls $z_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x}$ and enforcing
\begin{align}
    z_k - x_{k+1} &= 0\\
    f(x_k, u_k, z_k) &= 0
\end{align}
which leads to a specific structure in the stage-wise equality constraint jacobian, since the original equality do not depend on $z_k$
\begin{equation}
    H_u = \begin{bmatrix} M_1 & \\ M_2 & M_3 \end{bmatrix} \label{eq:Hu}
\end{equation}
where $M_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x \times n_x}$, $M_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{n_g \times n_x}$, and $M_3 \in \mathbb{R}^{n_g \times n_u}$.

Figure \ref{fig:lu_time_spent} shows the percentage of computation time of the Ricatti recursion spent in computing the LU factorization of the matrix shown in \eqref{eq:Hu} as a function of $n_x$, $n_u$, and $n_g$. Especially if $n_g$ and $n_u$ are large, a significant percentage of time is spent in the LU decomposition, motivating the need to perform this decomposition more efficiently. For small values of $n_x$, the decomposition is less significant.

\section{Blocked LU decomposition}
The structure in the matrix given by \eqref{eq:Hu} can be exploited by performing the decomposition more efficiently. More precisely, instead of computing
\begin{equation}
    P_l H_u P_r = L U
\end{equation}
directly, we compute
\begin{align}
    P_l^1 M_1 P_r^1 &= L_1 U_1 & P_l^3 M_3 P_r^3 &= L_3 U_3 \\
\end{align}

The blocked LU decomposition can be completed by writing
\begin{align}
    \begin{bmatrix} P_l^1 & \\ & P_l^1 \end{bmatrix}
    H_u
    \begin{bmatrix} P_r^1 & \\ & P_r^1 \end{bmatrix}
    &=
    \begin{bmatrix} L_1 & \\ L_2 & L_3 \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix} U_1 & U_2 \\ & U_3 \end{bmatrix}\\
    \longleftrightarrow
    \begin{bmatrix} P_l^1 M_1 P_r^1 & \\ P_l^2 M_2 P_r^1 & P_l^2 M_3 P_r^2 \end{bmatrix}
    &= \begin{bmatrix} L_1 U_1 & L_1 U_2 \\ L_2 U_1 & L_2 U_2 + L_3 U_3 \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}
where $U_2 = 0$ must hold, leading to
\begin{align}
    \begin{bmatrix} P_l^1 M_1 P_r^1 & \\ P_l^2 M_2 P_r^1 & P_l^2 M_3 P_r^2 \end{bmatrix}
    &= \begin{bmatrix} L_1 U_1 &  \\ L_2 U_1 & L_3 U_3 \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}
This means the blocked LU decomposition can be completed by computing
\begin{equation}
    L_2 = P_l^2 M_2 P_r^1 U_1^{-1}
\end{equation}
Note that $U_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x \times n_x}$ but is invertible only if $M_1$ is invertible. 



\subsection{Note on invertibility of $M_1$}
In the case where $M_1$ is not invertible, a more general approach is needed. Suppose $M_1$ is decomposed as 
\begin{align}
    P_l^1 M_1 P_r^1 &= L_1 U_1
\end{align}
where
\begin{align}
    L_1 &= \begin{bmatrix} K_1 & \\ K_2 & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} \end{bmatrix} & 
    &\text{and} &
    U_1 &= \begin{bmatrix} V_1 & V_2 \\ & \bm{0}_{n_x-\rho} \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}
Define 
\begin{align}
    \tilde{L} &\coloneqq \begin{bmatrix} K_1 & & \\ K_2 & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} & \\ & & \bm{I}_{n_g} \end{bmatrix}
    & &\text{such that} &
    \tilde{L}^{-1} &= \begin{bmatrix} K_1^{-1} & & \\ -K_2 K_1^{-1} & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} & \\ & & \bm{I}_{n_g} \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}
which leads to
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{L}^{-1} 
    \begin{bmatrix} P_l^1 & \\ & \bm{I}_{n_g} \end{bmatrix} 
    H_u
    \begin{bmatrix} P_r^1 & \\ & \bm{I}_{n_u} \end{bmatrix} 
    = \begin{bmatrix} V_1 & V_2 & \\ &  & \\ K_3 & K_4 & M_3 \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
where $\begin{bmatrix} K_3 & K_4 \end{bmatrix} = M_2 P_r^1$ with $K_3 \in \mathbb{R}^{n_g \times \rho}$ and $K_4 \in \mathbb{R}^{n_g \times n_x - \rho}$.

Continuing gaussian elimination, we can obtain
\begin{align}
    \hat{L} &\coloneqq \begin{bmatrix} K_1 & & \\ K_2 & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} & \\ K_3 V_1^{-1} & & \bm{I}_{n_g} \end{bmatrix}
    & &\text{such that} &
    \hat{L}^{-1} &= \begin{bmatrix} K_1^{-1} & & \\ -K_2 K_1^{-1} & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} & \\ -K_3 V_1^{-1} K_1^{-1} & & \bm{I}_{n_g} \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}
which leads to
\begin{equation}
    \hat{L}^{-1} 
    \begin{bmatrix} P_l^1 & \\ & \bm{I}_{n_g} \end{bmatrix} 
    H_u
    \begin{bmatrix} P_r^1 & \\ & \bm{I}_{n_u} \end{bmatrix} 
    = \begin{bmatrix} V_1 & V_2 & \\ &  & \\ & K_4 - K_3 V_1^{-1} V_2 & M_3 \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
Because of the zero-row, we must perform row pivoting in order to continue the decomposition. Hence, define
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{P} \coloneqq \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_\rho & & \\ & & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho}\\ & \bm{I}_{n_g} & \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
leading to
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{P}
    \hat{L}^{-1}
    \tilde{P}^\top \tilde{P} 
    \begin{bmatrix} P_l^1 & \\ & \bm{I}_{n_g} \end{bmatrix} 
    H_u
    \begin{bmatrix} P_r^1 & \\ & \bm{I}_{n_u} \end{bmatrix} 
    = \begin{bmatrix} V_1 & V_2 & \\ & K_4 - K_3 V_1^{-1} V_2 & M_3\\ &  & \\\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
using the fact that $\tilde{P}^\top \tilde{P} = \bm{I}_{n_x + n_g}$. Now, a remaining LU-decomposition should be performed on the matrix $\begin{bmatrix} K_4 - K_3 V_1^{-1} V_2 & M_3\end{bmatrix}$ such that
\begin{equation}
    P_l^2 \begin{bmatrix} K_4 - K_3 V_1^{-1} V_2 & M_3\end{bmatrix} P_r^2 = L_2 \begin{bmatrix} V_3 & V_4 \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
where $V_3 \in \mathbb{R}^{n_g \times n_x - \rho}$ and $V_4 \in \mathbb{R}^{n_g \times n_u}$. Note that if $\rho = n_x$, this decomposition reduces to a decomposition of $M_3$.

Finally, the decomposition of $M$ is given by 
\begin{equation}
    \hat{P}_l M \hat{P}_r = 
    \begin{bmatrix} K_1 & & \\ K_3 & L_2 & \\ K_2 &  & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix} V_1 & V_2 & \bm{0}_{\rho \times n_u}\\ & V_3 & V_4 \\ & & \bm{0}_{n_x-\rho \times n_u} \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
and 
\begin{align}
    \hat{P}_l &= 
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_\rho & & \\ & P_l^2 & \\ & & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_\rho & & \\ & & \bm{I}_{n_g} \\ & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} & \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix} P_l^1 & \\ & \bm{I}_{n_g} \end{bmatrix}\\
    \hat{P}_r &= \begin{bmatrix} P_r^1 & \\ & \bm{I}_{n_u}\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_\rho &\\ & P_r^2 \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

\begin{algorithm}
    \caption{Blocked LU decomposition of matrix $H_u$}
    \begin{algorithmic}
    \Require Matrix $H_u$ given by \eqref{eq:Hu}
    \Ensure LU decomposition with complete pivoting $P_l H_u P_r = L U$
    \State Compute $P_l^1$, $P_r^1$, $K_1$, $K_2$, $V_1$ and $V_2$ such that $P_l^1 M_1 P_r^1 = \begin{bmatrix}K1 & \\ K_2 & \bm{I}\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} V_1 & V_2\\ & \bm{0}\end{bmatrix}$
    \State $\tilde{M}_2 \gets M_2 P_r^1$
    \State $K_3 \gets \tilde{M}_2 \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_\rho \\ \bm{0}_{n_x-\rho \times \rho} \end{bmatrix} V_1^{-1}$
    \State $K_4 \gets \tilde{M}_2 \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_{\rho \times n_x-\rho} \\ \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} \end{bmatrix} - K_3 V_2$
    \State Compute $P_l^2$, $P_r^2$, $L_2$, $V_3$ and $V_4$ such that $P_l^2 \begin{bmatrix} K_4 & M_3 \end{bmatrix} P_r^2 = L_2 \begin{bmatrix} V_3 & V_4 \end{bmatrix}$
    \State $L \gets \begin{bmatrix} K_1 & & \\ K_3 & L_2 & \\ K_2 &  & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} \end{bmatrix}$
    \State $U \gets \begin{bmatrix} V_1 & V_2 & \bm{0}_{\rho \times n_u}\\ & V_3 & V_4 \\ & & \bm{0}_{n_x-\rho \times n_u} \end{bmatrix}$
    \State $P_l \gets \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_\rho & & \\ & P_l^2 & \\ & & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_\rho & & \\ & & \bm{I}_{n_g} \\ & \bm{I}_{n_x-\rho} & \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} P_l^1 & \\ & \bm{I}_{n_g} \end{bmatrix}$
    \State $P_r \gets \begin{bmatrix} P_r^1 & \\ & \bm{I}_{n_u}\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_\rho &\\ & P_r^2 \end{bmatrix}$

    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Numerical results} \FloatBarrier
Figure \ref{fig:scaling} shows the computation time of the full LU decomposition (direct) and the blocked LU decomposition for varying $n_x$, $n_u$, and $n_g$. Figure \ref{fig:scaling_relative} shows the relative computation time between the two methods. For small values of $n_x$, the blocked LU is slower than the full LU decomposition, but for other cases, a speedup of 20\% to 30\% is observed.

Figure \ref{fig:scaling_sliced} shows the relative difference in computation time as a function of $n_x$ and $n_u$ for different values of $n_g$. Again, for low values of $n_x$, the blocked LU is slower than the full LU decomposition. Additionally, the speedup is lower if $n_g$ is low.

Both of these observations are to be expected since for $n_g = 0$, $H_u$ simply becomes
\begin{equation}
    H_u = \begin{bmatrix} M_1 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation} 
If $n_u$ is then also small, both approaches just perform the same decomposition of $M_1$. Similarly, if $n_x$ is low, both approaches just perform the decomposition of $M_3$.

\section{Conclusion}
For some values of $n_x$, $n_u$ and $n_g$, the LU decomposition in the Ricatti recursion takes a significant percentage of the computation time. Hence, speeding it up can reduce the overall computation time. In the case of implicit integrators, the constraint jacobian (of which the LU decomposition is computed) is a 2x2 block matrix of which the top-left matrix is zero which can be exploited.

Numerical results show that the time required to compute the decomposition can be reduced by 20-40\%. It is not always faster to compute the blocked LU decomposition. However, the parameter values for which the blocked LU is slower correspond to parameter values where the LU decomposition is less important within the recursion.

However, in the case where the top-left matrix of the constraint jacobian (the $J$-matrix resulting from the dynamics) is not invertible, the blocked LU decomposition cannot be computed by first computing the LU decomposition of the top-left matrix and then re-using this decomposition to compute the full LU decomposition. Instead, pivoting within the full matrix should be included in the decomposition of the top-left block (which destroys structure).
